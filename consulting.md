# Biotech Commercialization and Technical Due Diligence

Bridging laboratory experience with business school frameworks to help investors, searchers, and biotech teams evaluate technical claims, assess operational friction, and plan commercial rollouts.

---

## Why This Perspective Matters

In life sciences, many of the most expensive mistakes happen when decisions are made without understanding both the science and the business context simultaneously:

* **Investors and Acquirers** frequently review impressive slide decks and high-level revenue projections, but lack the in-house scientific depth to interrogate whether an assay is truly reproducible, whether a protocol is too cumbersome for technicians, or whether key reagents are single-sourced from a vulnerable supplier.
* **Biotech Founders and Tool Developers** often have world-class technical mechanisms, but struggle to align product features with the purchasing realities of academic core labs, clinical diagnostics facilities, or pharmaceutical research teams.

Having spent a decade solving protein structures and developing assays as a Senior Scientist, combined with Executive MBA training in valuation, corporate finance, and market entry, I provide a grounded, independent perspective that bridges both worlds.

---

## Advisory Areas

### 1. Technical Due Diligence for Investors and Acquirers
*Targeted for Private Equity sponsors, search fund searchers, and family offices evaluating life science assets.*

An independent technical evaluation of scientific data, assay reproducibility, and operational feasibility before transactions:

* **Assay Robustness and Reproducibility:** Interrogating analytical validation reports, coefficient of variation (CV), limits of detection (LoD), and batch-to-batch consistency. Asking what controls were run and where the failure points occur.
* **Laboratory Workflow Friction:** Evaluating hands-on technician time, sample preparation complexity, automation compatibility, and training hurdles that can hinder customer adoption.
* **Technical Moat and Defensibility:** Examining whether an assay, instrument, or formulation has a durable technical barrier, or if a well-funded fast follower could easily replicate the chemistry.
* **Reagent Supply Chain and Scalability:** Identifying vulnerabilities such as single-sourced enzymes, custom antibodies, specialized polymers, or cleanroom bottlenecks that could impact scaling.

---

### 2. Commercialization and Go-To-Market Strategy
*Targeted for Biotech Founders, Tool Developers, and Growth Companies.*

Helping technical teams translate laboratory breakthroughs into viable commercial products with clear market fit:

* **Customer Persona and Value Proposition Alignment:** Aligning product messaging with the specific priorities of Academic PIs (publications and grant funding), Core Lab Directors (throughput, reliability, and unit economics), and Pharma R&amp;D (data integrity, compliance, and vendor stability).
* **Pricing Architecture and Unit Economics:** Modeling instrument and consumable pricing, evaluating reagent consumable pull-through rates, and structuring service agreements.
* **Distribution vs. Direct Sales Strategy:** Evaluating the trade-offs between partnering with established global scientific distributors versus building a dedicated direct technical sales force.
* **Voice-of-Customer (VoC) Discovery:** Conducting peer-to-peer technical interviews with bench researchers and laboratory managers to validate market demand before committing significant capital to production.
