## The Translation Bridge: From the Bench to Business Decisions

In biotechnology, significant friction occurs at the intersection of technical discovery and commercial execution. Commercial managers often lack the laboratory background to pressure-test scientific claims, while bench researchers frequently struggle to translate an assay mechanism into a scalable commercial model, pricing structure, or sales channel.

I am focused directly on bridging that gap.

As a Senior Scientist with a biochemistry PhD, I spent a decade solving protein structures, optimizing enzyme kinetics assays, and designing experiments. I understand how scientists evaluate data, what core laboratory directors require, and why certain technologies fail to gain adoption despite promising data at the bench.

Through my Executive MBA coursework, I have paired that scientific foundation with quantitative business frameworks:
* **Corporate Valuation and Finance:** Underwriting an aircraft program for Airbus, building leveraged buyout (LBO) models, and interrogating blended cost of capital (WACC) assumptions in corporate divestitures like Eaton's sale of hydraulics to Danfoss.
* **International Strategy and Market Entry:** Applying the CAGE distance framework and Porter's Diamond to analyze regulatory and distribution hurdles in emerging markets, including coursework on Abbott Laboratories and Avery Dennison in Vietnam, followed by cohort field study in Vietnam and Thailand.
* **Client Engagement:** Retained with my EMBA team by KCRW to redesign their membership model, learning firsthand how to align product value propositions around listener behavior rather than institutional assumptions.

The fundamental habit connecting both disciplines is the same: the discipline of asking the right question travels further than the subject matter it started in. Asking "what is actually being measured here?" applies equally to an enzyme kinetics assay and a corporate financial model.
