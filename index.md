## The Commercial Advantage: Leading from the Inside

In biotechnology and life science tools, the most common commercial pitfall is treating technical products like generic commodities. Commercial managers without laboratory literacy often struggle to articulate real customer value or identify adoption friction. At the same time, research teams often focus on technical mechanisms without understanding customer purchasing cycles, gross margin health, or distributor economics.

I lead directly from that intersection.

My commercial perspective is informed by ten years of laboratory bench experience solving protein structures and optimizing enzyme kinetics assays as a Senior Scientist with a biochemistry PhD. I understand the customer because I was the customer: I know how academic PIs evaluate reagents, what core laboratory directors require for throughput, and why certain technologies fail to gain traction despite promising preliminary data.

Through my Executive MBA coursework, I have paired that insider perspective with executive business disciplines:
* **Corporate Valuation and Finance:** Underwriting an aircraft program for Airbus, building leveraged buyout (LBO) models, and interrogating blended cost of capital (WACC) assumptions in corporate divestitures such as Eaton's sale of hydraulics to Danfoss.
* **International Strategy and Market Entry:** Applying the CAGE distance framework and Porter's Diamond to evaluate regulatory and distribution hurdles in emerging markets, including coursework on Abbott Laboratories and Avery Dennison in Vietnam, followed by cohort field study in Vietnam and Thailand.
* **Customer-Centric Execution:** Retained with my EMBA team by KCRW to redesign their membership model, learning firsthand how to align product value propositions around listener behavior rather than institutional assumptions.

The fundamental habit connecting both disciplines is the same: the discipline of asking the right question travels further than the subject matter it started in. Asking "what is actually being measured here?" applies equally to an enzyme kinetics assay and a corporate financial model.
